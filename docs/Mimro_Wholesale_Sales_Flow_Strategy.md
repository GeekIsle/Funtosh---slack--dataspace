# Mimro Originals Wholesale Sales Flow Strategy

Version 1.1 | November 6, 2026 | Owner: Mimro Wholesale Team

## Purpose and operating status

Turn suitable retail prospects into qualified buying conversations and wholesale orders through relevant product recommendations, professional outreach, and measured follow-up.

Current authorization: research and prepare drafts only. Do not send emails, contact prospects, or launch automated outreach. Catalogue references and button labels in drafts are preparation instructions; they do not mean a file has been attached or an email button has been implemented.

## 1. Combined intelligence model

This strategy adapts Panky Intel and the CHA/DEX/INT AI-agent attributes to wholesale sales. The sales roles below are an operational interpretation, not a claim that the original game framework defined a sales method.

| Component | Sales responsibility | Required output |
|---|---|---|
| Panky Intel | Signal → Analyze → Intervene → Learn | Next action, supporting evidence, outcome, and lesson |
| INT — Intelligence | Research prospects and evaluate product fit | Verified facts, relevant products, contact route, and uncertainties |
| CHA — Charisma | Communicate the offer clearly and personally | Relevant opening, practical value, and one clear question |
| DEX — Dexterity | Prepare materials and maintain execution discipline | Complete draft, attachment check, follow-up date, and accurate status |

Use these as responsibilities, whether one person or several tools perform the work. Numerical game attributes are not validated sales-performance scores.

## 3. Qualification and prioritization — INT

Start with prospects marked First review and Business-published in the source sheet. Review independent refill stores, sustainable goods shops, kitchenware retailers, and relevant home or bath boutiques first.

For each prospect:

1. Confirm the business is operating and its current assortment is relevant.
2. Identify the buying group. Combine branches under one purchasing approach where buying is shared.
3. Confirm the appropriate contact route. A public customer-service address is not automatically a purchasing contact.
4. Record one factual personalization detail with a source.
5. Select one or two lead products and explain the fit.
6. Mark the prospect Ready to draft, Needs verification, or Not a fit.

| Store type | Suggested starting products | Reason to explore fit |
|---|---|---|
| Refill / sustainable goods | Kitchen range and bath sets | Alignment with existing low-waste assortment |
| Kitchenware | Coconut cellulose sponges, loofah cellulose sponges, Swedish dishcloths | Everyday kitchen cleaning needs |
| Grocery / natural goods | Kitchen sponges and Swedish dishcloths | Potential household essentials addition |
| Bath / body | Natural loofah bath sets | Potential complement to soap and body-care products |
| Home / gifts | Swedish dishcloths and bath sets | Potential practical home and bath assortment |

These are hypotheses to verify, not evidence of demand or guaranteed sell-through.

## 4. Offer decision — Panky Intel

Before writing, answer: What evidence suggests this store is a fit, and what is the smallest useful next step?

| Signal | Decision | Next action |
|---|---|---|
| Clear assortment fit and appropriate contact | Prepare targeted introduction | Draft email with relevant products |
| Product fit but buyer unknown | Ask for routing | End with a purchasing-contact question |
| Outdated or conflicting business details | Hold | Reverify before drafting outreach |
| Several locations share purchasing | Coordinate | Prepare one buying-group approach |
| No credible product fit | Exclude | Record reason; do not force personalization |

Use approved wholesale information. Do not invent margins, minimum orders, lead times, stock availability, samples, free delivery, certifications, environmental claims, or marketplace terms.

## 5. Email construction — CHA

Target approximately 120–170 words, with short paragraphs suitable for mobile reading.

1. Subject: product relevance plus store name.
2. Opening: one verified fact about the store.
3. Introduction: Mimro, a Toronto-based business, and the relevant product range.
4. Fit: why one or two products may complement the assortment.
5. Evidence: attached catalogue, with only confirmed contents described.
6. Button: Explore Mimro Wholesale → https://www.mimro.com/pages/wholesale
7. Closing: one question matching the next action.
8. Signature: Mimro Wholesale Team, marketing@mimro.com, www.mimro.com.

Avoid generic praise, exaggerated sustainability language, unsupported performance claims, artificial urgency, and multiple competing requests. A Markdown link is a drafting placeholder; use a styled, accessible linked button in the eventual email editor, with a text-link fallback.

## 6. Preparation and review — DEX

Before marking a draft ready:

- Confirm the store name, contact route, buying group, source, and verification date.
- Check that the personalization detail is factual and the recommendation fits the business.
- Confirm all product names, prices, availability, and terms against approved current sources if mentioned.
- Select the correct catalogue; check that no internal prospect sheet is included as an attachment.
- Confirm the wholesale link, button destination, sender identity, and mobile readability.
- Remove placeholders and use one closing question.
- Record the draft as Draft ready, not Sent.

Sending requires a separate explicit instruction. After authorization, perform applicable contact-eligibility and opt-out checks and verify the actual attachments in the sending application.

## 7. Follow-up strategy — proposed cadence

This is a suggested future sequence, not a scheduled automation. Count business days from an actual authorized send date.

| Stage | Timing | Purpose | Suggested question |
|---|---|---|---|
| Introduction | Day 0 | Establish relevance and share catalogue | Open to reviewing a starter assortment? |
| Follow-up 1 | 4–5 business days later | Briefly surface the relevant product fit | Would kitchen essentials or bath products be more relevant? |
| Follow-up 2 | 5–7 business days after follow-up 1 | Resolve routing or timing | Is there a better contact or buying period? |
| Close the loop | 7–10 business days after follow-up 2 | End the sequence politely | Invite them to reconnect when relevant; no response required |

Do not repeat the same pitch. Stop the sequence on a reply, decline, opt-out, or invalid address. If a prospect requests a later date, record that date instead of continuing the default sequence. Silence is not evidence of interest. Do not automate this sequence under the current draft-only instruction.

## 8. Reply handling and next actions

| Response | Action |
|---|---|
| Interested | Ask about preferred categories and quantities; prepare an assortment using approved terms |
| Requests pricing | Provide the current approved catalogue or quote; clarify CAD and applicable terms |
| Requests samples | Check the approved sample policy before offering anything |
| Price concern | Ask which product or price point is the issue; recommend an appropriate option without inventing discounts |
| Existing supplier | Explore a specific assortment gap only if they are open to discussion |
| Wrong person | Request a referral and update the contact record |
| Not now | Record their stated timing; prepare a future follow-up only as authorized |
| Declines / opts out | Stop outreach and record the instruction |
| Bounce | Stop the sequence and verify the contact route |

## 9. Tracking structure

Maintain one record per buying group, with branch references where relevant.

Required fields: prospect ID; business name; city; store type; buying group; contact and role; contact source; verification date; verified personalization fact and source; proposed products; fit rationale; uncertainties; status; draft version; attachment version; last authorized send date; last response; next action; next action date; outcome; opt-out status.

Suggested statuses: Needs verification → Qualified → Draft ready → Approved to send → Sent → Replied → Assortment / quote → Order confirmed → Reorder review.

Alternate terminal statuses: Not a fit, Closed — no response, Declined, Opted out, Invalid contact. Never advance status without evidence of the corresponding event.

## 10. Learning loop and measures

Review each completed outreach batch by store type, lead products, and message version. Record the denominator for every rate.

- Qualified positive replies ÷ delivered introductions.
- Buying-contact referrals ÷ delivered introductions.
- Assortment or quote requests ÷ delivered introductions.
- Confirmed first orders ÷ qualified opportunities.
- Reorders ÷ first-order customers eligible for a reorder review.
- Bounces and opt-outs ÷ sent messages.

Treat link clicks and email opens as weak supporting signals; they do not establish buyer interest. Use replies and confirmed commercial outcomes to guide learning. Do not change strategy based on one result or present small samples as proof.

Panky review: What signal occurred? What did we infer? What action followed? What outcome is confirmed? What should change? Keep observations separate from hypotheses, and change one major message variable at a time when practical.
