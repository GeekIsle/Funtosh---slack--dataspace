Shopping “eco” can feel like a pop quiz you didn’t study for. One product says compostable. Another says biodegradable. Another says “natural,” but comes wrapped in plastic. Reviews disagree. Labels conflict. Suddenly you’ve spent 20 minutes comparing options for something you used to grab in 10 seconds.
That’s decision fatigue, and it’s one of the biggest reasons sustainable habits don’t stick.
Most people don’t avoid low-waste choices because they don’t care. They avoid them because the process feels complicated, expensive, and easy to get wrong. When you’re tired, your brain picks the fastest path, and good intentions turn into another cart full of convenience.
You don’t need perfect products. You need a simple approach you can use every time.
## The habit: the 3-Filter Method
Instead of hunting for the “best” option on the entire internet, run every choice through three quick filters:
1. **Repeat-use first.** Ask: “Will I use this again and again?” Repeat-use items cut waste without constant willpower, and mean fewer times you have to shop, compare, and restock.
2. **Fits my real routine.** Ask: “Is it easy to reach, easy to clean, and comfortable to use?” If it adds steps, it won’t become your default. The most sustainable product is the one you actually use.
3. **One category at a time.** Ask: “What’s my highest-repeat disposable habit this week?” Solve that one problem, then let the habit compound.
Try it for 7 days. Whenever you feel the urge to over-research, choose the simplest option that passes all three filters. Progress over perfection isn’t a slogan. It’s a strategy for consistency.
